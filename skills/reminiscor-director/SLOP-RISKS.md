# Slop risks and working rules

For each shot, check its beats against the catalogue below before writing its first frame or take. Write the guard into the frame and take prompts yourself, as a visible end state or a physical description. Tell the creator only about a risk you cannot guard, through the shot's notes ([`SHOTS.md`](SHOTS.md) § 6). Guarding is quiet work: no list of risks in the chat.

## The catalogue

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| Readable text and signs; brand-like objects | Keep text out of frame or unreadable ("a blank sign", "the label faces away"); describe objects generically |
| Hands, paws and tools (steering wheels, cups, envelopes) | Name the contact and where it stops: "the paw rests on the desk's edge and stays there"; one tool per hand |
| Liquids | Say where the liquid is and what it does in one beat: "the tea stays in the cup; only steam moves" |
| Subject count: extra people, duplicated characters | State the count from the fixed facts in `[Maintain Consistency]`: "exactly two racketeer dogs, no one else"; one handle per character |
| Props, furniture or cars that move, duplicate or teleport between frames | Every frame of a scene is an edit of its plate or the previous frame; positions from the fixed facts; "the desk and the chair stay where they are" |
| Lip-sync on the wrong speaker | One speaker per line, named with their marker right before the `{}` line; the others' mouths closed |
| Animals walking upright | "walks on all four paws" in every stage they move |
| Vehicle physics; the order of vehicles in a chase | Name which car leads in every stage; wheels on the road; a turn with its lean and slide |
| Characters without a sheet drifting between shots | Give every recurring character a sheet ([`SCRIPT.md`](SCRIPT.md) § 6) |
| Facial beats overdone into a mask (fangs, glow, snarl) | 2–4 small visible cues ("ears flatten, eyes narrow, lips stay closed"), never the emotion's name |
| Missing wardrobe (the lawman without trousers) | Wardrobe from the fixed facts, whole, in every prompt the character is in |
| "More natural" (no target) | A physical picture of the movement: what travels through the body, which way, how fast |
| Screen direction flipping between panels or shots (who is left, which way the car drives) | Write it into the fixed facts per scene ("the racketeers stay screen left, facing right") and into every prompt of that scene |
| A trait added mid-film (a scar, a hat, a torn coat) missing from earlier shots | Add it to the fixed facts and the reference description, then list the earlier shots whose frames and takes lack it, and ask whether to redo them |
| A prop changing between panels (a different envelope, phone or car) | One reference description per prop, word for word in every panel; a prop that recurs gets a sheet |
| A mirrored frame flipping only part of the picture | A flip moves everything on screen: characters, props, text, light and screen direction; flip the whole frame or none of it |

## Working rules

After Tarkovsky's *Sculpting in Time*:

1. **The script is a draft; the film is found in the material.** Frames fix composition and state, not performance. Judge a take by what it shows, the way you would judge an actor.
2. **Time pressure lives inside the shot.** One change per stage, room to breathe. Cramming beats is the main source of slop: split the take instead.
3. **The edit follows the takes.** In and out points are decided after watching, never in the storyboard.
4. **Observed detail.** Worn surfaces, real weather, objects with a history. Describe what is there, in plain words; quality buzzwords add nothing.
5. **State, not emotion.** Every face beat is 2–4 visible cues.
6. **No imitation.** A style is described by its visible traits, never by a director's or film's name.
